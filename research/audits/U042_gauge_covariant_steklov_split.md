## 2. Periodic slab and a nontrivial flat SU(2) background

Let

    Lambda_ell = {0,1,...,ell} x (Z/ell Z)^3

with open normal coordinate `r` and three periodic tangential coordinates. Consider the flat commuting background

    Ubar_0 = I,
    Ubar_j = exp[i h_j sigma_3 / ell],      j=1,2,3,          (1)

where the three `h_j` are fixed real holonomy angles. All plaquette holonomies are exactly the identity, while the tangential Wilson cycles are

    product_(x_j=0)^(ell-1) Ubar_j = exp(i h_j sigma_3).     (2)

Thus the cell can carry an `O(1)` global holonomy even though every background link differs from the identity by only `O(1/ell)`. This is exactly the type of collective cycle variable that U040-U041 insisted must not be discarded.

Use right-trivialized fluctuations

    U_e(t)=Ubar_e exp[i t a_e.sigma].                        (3)

Since every background plaquette is `I`, the first variation of the Wilson magnetic action vanishes. If `D_Ubar` denotes the linearized covariant curl, the second variation is exactly

    d^2/dt^2 S[U(t)]|_(t=0) = ||D_Ubar a||_2^2.             (4)

Indeed a plaquette has `U_p(t)=exp[i t F_p.sigma+O(t^2)]`, and

    1-(1/2)Tr U_p(t) = t^2 |F_p|^2/2 + O(t^3).

Equation (4) is three-color linearized Yang-Mills around a nontrivial flat background, not a scalar replacement of SU(2).

## 3. Exact covariant Fourier/Hodge decomposition

Complexify the adjoint color space relative to `sigma_3`. Its weights are

    w=0,+1,-1.

For tangential Fourier label `n=(n_1,n_2,n_3)` the covariant forward-difference symbol in weight `w` is

    p_(w,j)(n)
      = exp{ 2i[pi n_j + w h_j]/ell } - 1.                  (5)

After harmless unitary phase rotations, only its real magnitude matters. Define

    lambda_w(n)
      = sum_j |p_(w,j)(n)|^2
      = 4 sum_j sin^2([pi n_j+w h_j]/ell).                  (6)

Let `p` be the corresponding three-component symbol. The tangential curl matrix is the cross-product matrix `C(p)`. Exactly,

    C(p)^* C(p) = lambda I - p p^*.                          (7)

Hence, whenever `p != 0`, the tangential one-form space splits orthogonally into

    span{p}          (covariant gradient),
    p^perp           (two transverse/coexact polarizations),

with curl eigenvalues `0` and `lambda`, respectively. When `p=0`, all three tangential components are covariantly harmonic cycle directions.

The checker verifies (7) exactly on rational symbols; the proof is the three-dimensional vector identity `|p x a|^2=lambda|a|^2-|p^*a|^2`.

## 4. The unreduced boundary soft sector is surface-sized

This is the first important correction to a naive reading of U041.

For an arbitrary Lie-algebra scalar `phi(r,x)`, the linearized gauge fluctuation

    a = D_Ubar phi                                           (8)

has zero curvature by `D_Ubar^2=0` on the flat background. Its two boundary tangential traces are

    p phi_0,        p phi_ell.                               (9)

For every Fourier/color mode with `p != 0`, the two boundary scalar values `phi_0` and `phi_ell` are independent. Therefore the two-dimensional longitudinal boundary subspace is an **exact zero-energy kernel** of the full Maxwell/Yang-Mills Steklov form.

Conversely, these exhaust the kernel. In tangential Fourier space, zero curvature at `p != 0` forces the tangential component to be parallel to `p`, hence to be a covariant gradient; at `p=0`, zero normal curvature forces the tangential component to be independent of `r`, leaving only the three diagonal harmonic cycles. This is the discrete `H^1(T^3 x I)` statement proved mode-by-mode, not an appeal to continuum topology.

At the identity background, per real color, there are `ell^3-1` nonzero tangential Fourier modes. Exact boundary gradients therefore contribute

    2(ell^3-1)

zero directions. At zero momentum the three diagonal harmonic traces `(b,b)` extend constantly through the slab and also have zero curvature. Thus the exact kernel dimension per real color is

    dim ker Q_boundary = 2(ell^3-1)+3 = 2 ell^3+1.          (10)

For SU(2) at the identity background this is

    6 ell^3+3.                                               (11)

So the statement

    "all boundary modes with stiffness O(1/ell) form a bounded-dimensional space"

is FALSE before gauge separation. The zero-energy gauge orbit itself has surface-order dimension.

This does **not** license projecting each phase cell to a gauge singlet. U037 already showed that such a projection can remove globally physical Wilson-loop states. The correct conclusion is instead that boundary gauge-orbit/representation data must be carried algebraically through gluing, while the magnetic Steklov split is applied to the quotient/coexact directions.

## 5. Exact two-boundary Steklov matrix on the coexact sector

Fix one transverse polarization and one Fourier/color mode with tangential eigenvalue `lambda>=0`. After quotienting the gradient direction, its normal profile is scalar. For endpoint values `(a,b)` minimize

    E(u)=1/2 sum_(r=0)^(ell-1) |u_(r+1)-u_r|^2
         +lambda/2 sum_(r=0)^ell |u_r|^2,                   (12)

subject to `u_0=a`, `u_ell=b`.

Write

    cosh gamma = 1 + lambda/2.                               (13)

The exact boundary quadratic form is

    E_min(a,b)=1/2 (a,b) Q_ell(lambda) (a,b)^T,             (14)

with

    Q_ell(lambda)
      = [[q,-r],[-r,q]],                                     (15)

    q = lambda/2 + sinh(gamma)coth(ell gamma),
    r = sinh(gamma)csch(ell gamma).                          (16)

The symmetric and antisymmetric face combinations have stiffnesses

    q_-(lambda)
      = lambda/2+sinh(gamma)tanh(ell gamma/2),               (17)

    q_+(lambda)
      = lambda/2+sinh(gamma)coth(ell gamma/2).               (18)

At `lambda=0` the continuous limits are

    q_-(0)=0,       q_+(0)=2/ell.                            (19)

The zero in (19) is the diagonal harmonic cycle mode; the antisymmetric harmonic cycle has the expected `2/ell` stiffness.

Exact finite Schur complements for rational `lambda` and `ell=1,...,6` are checked in `checks/U042_gauge_covariant_steklov_checks.py`. Wolfram separately corroborated (7), (17)-(19), and the scaling exponent used below.

## 6. Uniform bound on the gauge-quotiented `O(1/ell)` soft sector

Fix `C>0`. A transverse mode is called Steklov-soft if either parity stiffness satisfies

    q_-(lambda) <= C/ell

or

    q_+(lambda) <= C/ell.                                   (20)

Since `q_+>=q_-`, it suffices to count modes satisfying the first inequality.

For `lambda>0`, put

    x=ell gamma/2.

From `sinh gamma>=gamma`, (17) gives

    q_-(lambda) >= gamma tanh(ell gamma/2)
                    = (2x/ell)tanh x.                       (21)

Thus (20) implies

    2x tanh x <= C.                                         (22)

For `x>=1`, `2 tanh x>1`, so (22) implies `x<C`; if `x<1` it is already bounded. Hence

    x <= R_C := max(1,C).                                   (23)

For `ell>=2R_C`, `gamma<=1`, and

    lambda=2(cosh gamma-1) <= 2 gamma^2
           <= 8 R_C^2/ell^2.                               (24)

Now choose each shifted tangential phase modulo `pi ell` so that

    d_j=dist(pi n_j+w h_j, pi ell Z) in [0,pi ell/2].        (25)

Equation (6) and `sin y>=2y/pi` on `[0,pi/2]` imply

    lambda_w(n)
      >= 16/(pi^2 ell^2) sum_j d_j^2.                       (26)

Combining (24)-(26), every soft mode satisfies

    sum_j d_j^2 <= (pi^2/2) R_C^2.                          (27)

Choose the adjoint holonomy representatives modulo the center so that `|h_j|<=pi/2`. Writing `m_j=n_j-ell k_j` for the nearest wrap, (27) gives

    |m_j| <= R_C/sqrt(2)+1/2.                               (28)

Therefore, for each adjoint weight, only finitely many integer triples `m` can occur, with the explicit crude count

    N_C <= [2 ceil(R_C/sqrt(2)+1/2)+1]^3.                   (29)

Each nonzero covariant momentum has two transverse polarizations and two face parities. Allowing all three adjoint weights and overcounting possible harmonic coincidences gives, for all sufficiently large `ell`,

    dim soft_coexact <= 12 N_C + 18.                         (30)

The finitely many `ell<2R_C` can be absorbed into a larger constant depending only on `C`. Thus there is a finite

    D(C)<infinity                                             (31)

such that the real gauge-quotiented coexact/harmonic soft sector has dimension at most `D(C)` for **every `ell` and every flat commuting holonomy `(h_1,h_2,h_3)`**.

This is the first genuinely gauge-covariant bounded-soft-sector theorem in the project. Its scope is still the linearized Hessian about flat commuting SU(2) backgrounds on the periodic slab.

## 7. Why arbitrary flat holonomy matters

The theorem is not merely the identity-background Fourier calculation. The holonomies (2) remain `O(1)` as `ell->infinity`; only their link representatives are `O(1/ell)`. Charged adjoint modes experience the shifts

    pi n_j -> pi n_j +/- h_j.                                (32)

The count (31) is uniform in those shifts. Therefore coarse cycle holonomy does not create an extensive family of new magnetically soft coexact modes in this flat sector.

This is directly compatible with U037's warning: cycle variables are retained, but they occupy a controlled coarse sector rather than being set to the identity.

## 8. Nonlinear curved-background perturbations: the scale is favorable

A complete U040 regular core is not generally flat. We therefore need to know whether the nonlinear Hessian can perturb the high Steklov sector without closing its `1/ell` boundary stiffness.

There is a useful local estimate that is independent of the cell volume. In any fixed logarithmic chart `max_e |A_e|<=delta_0`, smoothness of the four-link Wilson plaquette function gives a universal constant `K_H` such that

    |H_A[a,a]-H_0[a,a]|
       <= K_H delta sum_e |a_e|^2,                           (33)

where `delta=max_e|A_e|`. The reason is local: the plaquette Hessian is Lipschitz on a fixed compact chart, and every four-dimensional edge belongs to at most six plaquettes. Equation (33) is an absolute Hessian estimate, not yet a relative coercivity theorem because gauge/low modes can make `H_0` small.

The one-dimensional trace estimate explains the needed amplification. For any sequence `u_0,...,u_ell`,

    sum_(r=0)^ell |u_r|^2
      <= 2(ell+1)|u_0|^2
         +2 ell(ell+1) sum_(r=0)^(ell-1)|u_(r+1)-u_r|^2.   (34)

If its boundary data lie in a linear sector whose Steklov stiffness is at least `C_0/ell`, then the endpoint norm is bounded by the minimized energy times `O(ell/C_0)`. Equations (33)-(34) consequently amplify a local `O(delta)` coefficient error by at most `O(delta ell^2)` in the corresponding scalar/coexact slab form.

U041's optimized regular-core exponents give

    delta = O(alpha^(7/26)),
    ell   = O(alpha^(-1/13)),

so

    delta ell^2 = O(alpha^(3/26)) -> 0.                     (35)

This is important: there is **no scaling obstruction** to perturbing the stiff Steklov sector from the flat theorem. The same optimized exponents leave room after the natural trace/Poincare loss.

What is NOT yet proved is the required full nonabelian analogue of (34) on the curved phase cell after separating boundary gauge representations and allowing mode mixing. Establishing that gauge-equivariant trace/coercivity estimate, and then applying (33), is the first unresolved implication.

# U009 — boundary-compatible excess action: counterexample to the naive volume-excess formulation

Date: 2026-09-14.

**We have not solved the Yang–Mills problem yet.**

This unit tests U008's proposed next lemma. The result is negative: with completely arbitrary fixed boundary data, a positive density of large-curvature interior plaquettes does **not** force a volume-order excess action above the boundary-compatible minimum. The deterministic volume deficit from the identity remains true, but it cannot be upgraded to the proposed uniform excess statement without restricting boundary data or redefining the event.

## 1. Proposed statement under test

Let B be a four-dimensional block, fix all boundary links, and minimize the Wilson deficit

    A_B(U)=sum_(p in B) [1-(1/2) Re Tr U_p]

over interior links compatible with that boundary. U008 asked whether the additional condition

    at least rho of interior plaquettes have class angle >= epsilon

necessarily raises the action by c n^4 above this boundary-compatible minimum, uniformly over all boundary links.

That universal statement is false.

## 2. Immediate variational counterexample principle

Choose any boundary condition for which **an action minimizer itself already belongs to the bad event**. Then

    inf_{bad, boundary fixed} A_B
      = inf_{all, boundary fixed} A_B,

so the excess action is exactly zero, not c n^4.

Thus it suffices to exhibit boundary data whose minimizing extension has positive-density interior curvature.

## 3. Explicit abelian-embedded SU(2) construction

Use the maximal torus generated by H=diag(i,-i) and a rectangular block. Choose link variables in this commuting U(1) subgroup with a constant lattice field strength in one coordinate plane. In additive angle notation one may take, away from the wrap/boundary convention,

    A_2(x)=phi x_1,
    A_mu(x)=0 for mu != 2,

so every (1,2)-plaquette has holonomy

    U_12 = exp(i phi sigma_3)

and class angle |phi| (mod the principal interval), while other oriented planes are flat. Fix the boundary links to the restriction of this configuration.

For sufficiently small |phi|<pi/2, the Wilson plaquette energy 1-cos(theta) is strictly convex in the abelian plaquette angle on the relevant branch. Among abelian configurations with the same boundary circulation/total flux through each (1,2) sheet, Jensen's inequality makes the constant-curvature field minimize the sum of plaquette energies on each sheet. Consequently its action cannot be lowered within the abelian sector by redistributing the prescribed flux.

This already invalidates any argument that tries to obtain a **uniform** volume excess from the bad-event definition using only local plaquette convexity and arbitrary boundary data: the natural boundary-compatible reference can itself carry extensive curvature.

A fully nonabelian proof that this configuration is the global SU(2) minimizer for every such boundary choice is not needed to expose the logical flaw in the proposed route. More generally, by definition, if one chooses boundary data from any global constrained minimizer that has the prescribed positive-density property, the excess is zero. The missing issue is existence of such minimizers with the property; constant-flux torus embeddings provide the natural explicit family and show why arbitrary boundary curvature cannot be ignored.

## 4. Stronger formulation that survives the counterexample

The bad event must be measured **relative to a boundary-compatible low-action background**, not relative to the identity. Let U^*(boundary) be a selected minimizer or controlled local background. A viable event would require an excess curvature/energy condition such as

    A_B(U)-A_B(U^*) >= tau n^4

or a positive density of plaquettes whose holonomy differs from the corresponding background plaquette by at least epsilon in a gauge-invariant relative metric.

For the first definition the volume excess is tautological, so the hard work shifts to proving that the event has a useful geometric characterization and that its complement supports the small-field transfer estimates. For the second definition one needs a stability theorem: distance from the minimizer manifold must control action excess modulo gauge and topological zero modes.

This is a more faithful weak-coupling problem. Arbitrary boundary data can impose nonzero classical fields; a continuum construction must expand around the appropriate background/minimizer rather than insist every block be close to the identity.

## 5. Collar scaling

A one-cell collar does not fix the conceptual issue. For an n^4 block, removing one cell from each side leaves an (n-2)^4 interior. The collar cell count is

    n^4-(n-2)^4 = 8n^3-24n^2+32n-16,

which is surface order O(n^3), while the interior still has 6(n-2)^4 plaquettes. Wolfram 15.0.1 independently expanded these counts and verified the interior fraction tends to one.

Thus a collar can isolate boundary effects geometrically, but it cannot by itself guarantee that the interior reference field is flat. Boundary-imposed flux can penetrate the interior unless a separate estimate shows screening/local relaxation. Assuming such screening would risk assuming the very infrared behavior under investigation.

## 6. Consequence for the route

Reject the U008 universal statement:

    arbitrary boundary + positive density of absolute large plaquette angles
       => volume excess above boundary minimum.

It is false as a proof architecture because the boundary-compatible minimizer may itself be an absolute large-field configuration. Preserve instead:

- U008's absolute action deficit from the identity;
- the isolated free-boundary entropy estimate;
- U007's scaling criterion for a genuinely growing collective excess cost.

The next route must work relative to a minimizer/background manifold and handle its gauge/topological degeneracies.

## 7. Next executable step

Formulate and test a **local stability inequality around a boundary-compatible minimizer**. In a small-field abelianized model, the Wilson action Hessian is a discrete curl-curl operator. After quotienting gauge gradients, determine whether a Poincare-type inequality gives a positive lower bound on action excess in terms of distance from the minimizer, and track how the smallest nonzero eigenvalue scales with block side n. If it decays like n^-2, quantify whether the resulting volume-distance cost is still sufficient for U007 after choosing the block scale n(a). This is the next precise mathematical bottleneck.
